# Samples, provenance, and visualization

Real protocol data is unusually important for STANAG 4607 work. A parser can
agree perfectly with its own encoder while still disagreeing with deployed
producers over padding, older editions, masks, packet grouping, transport, or
fields that are technically invalid but common in practice. For that reason,
this project distinguishes four kinds of evidence:

1. authoritative vectors, when an issuing authority identifies them as such;
2. independently produced or captured streams, which provide interoperability
   evidence but are not normative;
3. synthetic packets, which isolate specific fields and boundary conditions;
4. deliberately malformed data, which proves rejection and recovery behavior.

No sample, including one marked “real” in its packet header, establishes NATO
certification. Exact URLs, revisions, hashes, byte counts, acquisition dates,
and redistribution decisions are recorded in
`references/data/manifest.json`.

## Edition A Version 1 synthetic interoperability packets

The repository includes eight small, unchanged Version 4.1 packets under
`tests/fixtures/`. They came from the Apache-2.0
[`Decent-Cybersecurity/synapsecommand-public`](https://github.com/Decent-Cybersecurity/synapsecommand-public)
repository at commit
`5ab80f5fa93895722e587f755e7de446b71feb5b`. They were generated by a separate
implementation, making them more valuable than fixtures produced by this
library itself, but they remain synthetic rather than operational radar data.

Together they exercise high-resolution target locations, a full Dwell existence
mask, Free Text, Test and Status, Processing History, Platform Location,
repeated Mission context, opaque registered-extension segments, and HRR fixed
metadata. Their small size makes them appropriate for deterministic tests,
arbitrary input chunking, lossless round trips, and error-localization tests.

Inspect and visualize a bundled target-bearing packet:

```console
stanag4607 inspect tests/fixtures/mission_dwell_hi_res_targets.gmti
stanag4607 validate tests/fixtures/mission_dwell_hi_res_targets.gmti
stanag4607 geojson tests/fixtures/mission_dwell_hi_res_targets.gmti > targets.geojson
stanag4607 demo tests/fixtures/mission_dwell_hi_res_targets.gmti
```

The demo replays the sample continuously through the incremental decoder and
validators at `http://127.0.0.1:8768/`. To see live diagnostics rather than an
all-valid stream, use `free_text_and_test_status.gmti` or
`hrr_signature_parked_both_time_branches.gmti`. Those fixtures intentionally
surface packet-context and HRR-continuity findings under the current profile.

Additional upstream packets can be downloaded without relying on a moving
branch:

```console
revision=5ab80f5fa93895722e587f755e7de446b71feb5b
base=https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Decent-Cybersecurity/synapsecommand-public/$revision/packages/cdm/synapse_cdm/fixtures/gmti
curl -fLO "$base/sparse_mask_minimum_dwell.gmti"
curl -fLO "$base/delta_targets_across_the_prime_meridian.gmti"
```

### Why these packets matter

They are the current-edition baseline. They let tests pin down exact Edition A
Version 1 behavior without requiring a large or sensitive radar recording, and
they cover masks and optional-field combinations that a short operational
capture may never happen to contain. Their limitation is equally important:
agreement with another synthetic implementation cannot prove compatibility with
a fielded sensor.

## Wireshark issue 19566 raw stream and UDP capture

In January 2024, a Wireshark user opened
[issue 19566](https://gitlab.com/wireshark/wireshark/-/issues/19566) because
STANAG 4607 data carried over UDP could not be selected with Wireshark's
“Decode As” workflow.
The report attached both the raw `gmti.s4607` byte stream and
`gmti_udp.pcap`, a packet capture of that same stream transported over UDP to
port 12345. This paired form is rare and especially useful: concatenating the 26
UDP payloads reproduces the 5,811-byte raw stream exactly.

Observed contents of the raw stream:

- Version ID `30`;
- 26 packets containing one Mission, six Job Definition, and 19 Dwell segments;
- 193 target reports spanning `2017-05-11T17:04:08.083Z` through
  `2017-05-11T17:04:12.563Z`;
- target coordinates covering roughly 45.17–45.68° N and
  76.36–75.66° W, around the Ottawa–Gatineau region;
- platform ID `C-FNRC`, sensor-model bytes `OSP30G`, sensor type 33, and radar
  mode 137; and
- P7 exercise indicator 0, which declares operation/real data in this edition.

The platform identifier is consistent with the National Research Council of
Canada's Convair 580 research aircraft. [NRC describes that
aircraft](https://www.nrc.canada.ca/en/research-development/nrc-facilities/research-aircraft-fleet)
as a flying laboratory supporting spotlight synthetic-aperture radar,
navigation, EO/IR, and other remote-sensing work. That relationship makes the
sample plausible, but it is an inference rather than provenance supplied by the
uploader.

The PCAP was recorded in January 2024 while its internal radar observations are
dated May 2017, so it appears to be a replay used to demonstrate the networking
problem rather than a live 2024 collection. The issue does not name the radar,
producer software, acquisition chain, or data license. The project therefore
describes it as an independently submitted stream marked operation/real, not as
an authenticated operational or official vector.

Download and inspect it:

```console
mkdir -p samples/private/wireshark
curl -fL -o samples/private/wireshark/gmti.s4607 \
  https://gitlab.com/wireshark/wireshark/uploads/949a79235956ad037977ef3441eeddc9/gmti.s4607
curl -fL -o samples/private/wireshark/gmti_udp.pcap \
  https://gitlab.com/wireshark/wireshark/uploads/b87ec482b7b3895b878f8b5755035ffc/gmti_udp.pcap
stanag4607 inspect samples/private/wireshark/gmti.s4607
stanag4607 geojson samples/private/wireshark/gmti.s4607 > targets.geojson
```

### Why this capture matters

It exercises several boundaries that synthetic packet files do not: actual UDP
datagram framing, many target reports per dwell, changing jobs, plausible
geospatial motion, and older producer behavior. The library currently decodes
the file from arbitrary 17-byte chunks and re-encodes every packet byte for
byte. The strict Edition A validator separately reports six P10 findings on
packets without Dwell or HRR data. The explicit
`LEGACY_VERSION_30_JOB_DEFINITION` profile recognizes only these Version 3.0
Job Definition-only packets when J1 equals P10. The default remains strict, and
this observed producer convention is not generalized into Edition 3 conformance.

## AFRL Ten Targets public-release exercise corpus

The Java [`bradh/jim`](https://github.com/bradh/jim) project contains two files
that [its fixture
README](https://github.com/bradh/jim/blob/003999a608dbe4d3bc1c297b8f4a7f997a6385ff/s4607/src/test/resources/README.md)
identifies as AFRL public-release STANAG 4607 data:

- `pr_scenario_10tgts_situationAwarenessMask.4607`; and
- `pr_scenario_10tgts_precisionTargetingMaskPlusRCS.4607`.

The files were added to that repository in 2021. The original AFRL download
location is no longer identified, and the mirror repository does not declare a
license, so the files are retained locally rather than committed here. An
[archived JITC GMTI site](https://web.archive.org/web/20171005074254/http://jitc.fhu.disa.mil/projects/mti/forms/testdatareq.aspx)
confirms that test data was historically distributed through a request process,
which helps explain why the original corpus is difficult to locate directly
today.

Both profiles contain 194 Version 3.0 packets: 20 Mission segments, 20 Job
Definition segments, 154 Dwell segments, and one Free Text segment. They span
about nine minutes and 44 seconds and contain 218 target reports. The packet
headers use exercise indicator 129, explicitly identifying exercise/simulated
data and platform `Test Data`.

The profiles describe the same scenario at different reporting fidelities:

- **Situational Awareness** uses reduced target-location encoding and omits
  target classification and radar-cross-section detail.
- **Precision Targeting plus RCS** uses high-resolution target locations and
  carries the additional classification/RCS-oriented fields selected by its
  mask.

The JIM project also contains `_cleaned` versions. The originals use NUL bytes
where the Basic Character Set requires space padding. The cleaned variants
replace that padding so JIM can test an exact decode/encode round trip. We keep
both because they serve different purposes: originals test tolerant ingestion
and diagnostics, while cleaned files test standards-oriented writing without
discarding the scenario.

### Why the AFRL corpus matters

The paired profiles provide a controlled comparison of compact and detailed
reporting for the same moving targets. Their duration, repeated mission/job
cycles, and 154 dwells are large enough to exercise contextual decoding,
continuity, UI playback, map trails, target density, and performance in a way
that isolated packets cannot. They also preserve real historical
interoperability lessons: public test data can contain character-padding and
profile issues even when widely used by other implementations.

All four versions decode incrementally and round-trip byte-for-byte. Under the
current Edition A validation profile, the original files correctly raise Basic
Character Set diagnostics, while the cleaned files remove those character
errors. Other findings, including P10 usage and reduced-location scale-factor
conditions, remain visible rather than being hidden by compatibility guesses.

## Pentland Edge Edition 3.1 sample

`pentlandedge/s4607_extra` publishes `list1.4607` under Apache-2.0. It is a
small, 188-byte Version 3.1 synthetic stream used by the independent Erlang
implementation. It is too small to establish broad compatibility, but it gives
us a second producer and a distinct older Version ID for framing, dispatch, and
future backward-compatibility tests.

## Wireshark issue 8962 constructed Version 2.1 data

Wireshark [issue
8962](https://gitlab.com/wireshark/wireshark/-/issues/8962), opened while
native STANAG 4607 file support was being added, includes `bogus-data.4607`.
It contains six Version 2.1 packets and is
explicitly named and described as example/bogus data. It must never be presented
as radar evidence. Its value is narrower: testing file-type recognition,
historical framing, malformed assumptions, and tolerant lossless preservation.

## Evidence and redistribution rules

Use the samples according to the question being tested:

| Question | Best evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Current Edition A field behavior | Version 4.1 synthetic interoperability packets |
| UDP carriage and packet extraction | Wireshark raw/PCAP pair |
| Longer contextual and visualization workflow | AFRL Ten Targets profiles |
| Tolerant handling of deployed or historical quirks | Original AFRL files and Wireshark capture |
| Old-version framing only | Pentland Edge and Wireshark `bogus-data.4607` |
| Current operational producer conformance | Not yet available |

Always record the source URL, immutable revision where available, license or
rights uncertainty, byte size, and SHA-256 before relying on new data. Do not
commit operational, restricted, export-controlled, personally sensitive, or
unclearly redistributable captures. A local copy and a public URL do not by
themselves grant redistribution rights.
